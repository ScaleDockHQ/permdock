title: "0027: Governance-first Cloud"
description: PermDock Cloud leads with the decision log sold as compliance evidence and agent governance; the SCIM receiver moves from considered to scheduled as an open-source entry (permdock/scim) with a hosted relay; the approval inbox stays a feature; meters are active principals, connected tenants, resolved approvals and retention; every Cloud connector is a standard wire format; the company positions for an acquirer.

## Status

Accepted, September 2026. Superseded in part by [0042](/docs/decisions/0042-cloud-identity-gateway) (authentication). Reorders the Cloud sequencing recorded in [0021](/docs/decisions/0021-embedded-pdp-hosted-ads) without changing its architecture; moves the SCIM receiver out of "considered and set aside" on the [commercial landscape](/docs/research/commercial-landscape); resolves the sampling question on [audit and observability](/docs/concepts/audit-and-observability) and two open questions on the [Cloud adapter](/docs/adapters/cloud) page. The governance-first sequencing, SCIM relay, meters and invariant 15 still stand.

## Context

[0021](/docs/decisions/0021-embedded-pdp-hosted-ads) fixed the shape: an embedded PDP that never needs the network, and an optional hosted control plane behind three interfaces (`ApprovalStore`, `DecisionSink`, `SnapshotSource`). It sequenced the Cloud as "approvals and the decision log first" and made the approval inbox the lead capability because Eve's documentation pointed at exactly that gap.

A second look at the market in September 2026 changed the emphasis, not the shape:

- **Standalone authorization has a poor exit record.** Aserto shut down in May 2025 with a post-mortem that describes a SaaS control plane managing authorizers in customer environments and says the economics did not add up; that is the 0021 architecture. Styra was acqui-hired by Apple and its commercial products open-sourced. Warrant was absorbed into WorkOS and its standalone FGA product deprecated. Oso deprecated its library and pivoted to agent monitoring. The survivors (Cerbos, Permit, Oso) are small, and Cerbos's own path from PDP to Hub to an "enterprise authorization management platform" shows the control plane is paid for by compliance buyers, not by developers.
- **Approvals are commoditising.** A durable approval API with an audit log is sold for a flat 99 USD per month (Pushary), given away (Rills), or shipped as an SDK. The defensible part of PermDock's approvals is not the inbox; it is re-running `decide` and recomputing the bound `token` before trusting a stored approval, and the distinct-approver rule ([0022](/docs/decisions/0022-approvals-are-pluggable)).
- **Two buyers pay.** Enterprise procurement pays for SSO, SCIM and audit evidence (WorkOS reached about 30 million USD ARR on those). Security teams pay to govern agents (Okta reports agentic products at 30 percent of new bookings, and surveys report most companies have agents in production and few are confident they are governed). Neither buys "a permissions library"; both buy "who can do what, prove it, and let me stop it". PermDock already produces the two artefacts they need: a decision event carrying `principal`, `actor`, `delegation`, `tenant` and `outcome`, and a bound approval token.
- **Authentication and gateways are the wrong broadening.** Authentication is commoditised by free open source (Better Auth, acquired by Vercel), consolidating (Twilio bought Stytch for roughly its raised capital), already present on the Vercel Marketplace (Clerk, Auth0), and operationally heavy in exactly the way [0018](/docs/decisions/0018-authentication-is-upstream) rejected. Identity-aware and MCP gateways are won by whoever owns the network path or the IdP, and a gateway sees strings where PermDock sees the row. Every `subjectFrom*` provider is also a plausible acquirer or co-marketer; building against them forfeits that.

The one enterprise feature that fits the architecture and that buyers already pay for elsewhere is directory group-to-role provisioning. 0021 kept a SCIM receiver off the roadmap because the open-source path (map in the IdP, or sync into your own table) covers the need and because the Cloud must not resolve memberships (invariant 15). Both remain true, and both are compatible with an open-source receiver that writes to a store the application owns.

## Decision

1. **The decision log leads.** PermDock Cloud's first paid capability is the decision log presented as compliance evidence and agent governance: access-review queries per principal, actor, tenant and window; agent-activity views; the approval chain joined on `token`; signed exports (`permdock-decisions+jwt`), OCSF forwarding and CSV for compliance platforms; retention tiers. The word "audit" describes the mechanism ([audit and observability](/docs/concepts/audit-and-observability)); "evidence" and "governance" describe what is sold. Signed decision-batch export moves from Phase 3 to Phase 2 so the alpha ships evidence with provenance.
2. **Every event is written by default.** `DecisionSink` implementations receive all `granted`, `denied` and `approval-required` events; sampling (`sample: { granted: 0.1 }`) is opt-in. Access reviews are about what was allowed, so a log that samples grants is not evidence.
3. **Governance actions never decide.** The Cloud may reject pending approvals for an actor or a principal and may invalidate snapshots through `SnapshotSource`; it cannot deny a future check. A "freeze this agent" action is those two operations plus a recommendation that the application's own `RoleSource` or `context` read a kill-switch table it owns. Invariant 15 is unchanged.
4. **The SCIM receiver is scheduled, as open source first.** A new entry `permdock/scim` (Phase 2) exports `scimHandler` (an RFC 7644 Fetch handler for `/Users` and `/Groups`), a `DirectoryStore` interface with `memoryDirectoryStore()`, and `directoryMembershipSource(store)`, which turns the synced groups into a `MembershipSource` (`{ tenant, team: group.id, roles, via: 'group:<id>' }`, no memberships for a deactivated user). Group-to-role mapping is application data: a `groupRoles` map or a `roles` attribute under the PermDock SCIM schema extension `urn:permdock:scim:schemas:extension:roles:1.0` on `Group`. The endpoint authenticates the caller with a per-tenant static bearer (`token`, compared against a stored hash, which is what Okta and Entra ID support) or a `TokenVerifier` (`verifier`, RFC 7523 JWT bearer). The tenant is bound to the credential and never read from a body.
5. **The Cloud hosts a relay, not a membership source.** PermDock Cloud runs a hosted SCIM endpoint per connected tenant with an IdP setup wizard, a group-to-role mapping UI and a sync log, and replays each provisioning operation to the application's `scimHandler` authenticated with an RFC 7523 JWT bearer signed by the Cloud's per-environment key (verified against the same JWKS as signed snapshots). The store the application owns stays authoritative; the Cloud never implements `MembershipSource` or `RoleSource`, and a Cloud outage leaves memberships at their last synced state. No new JWS `typ` value is introduced.
6. **The inbox stays, priced as a feature.** `ApprovalStore`, the inbox UI and delivery are unchanged. The meters are: monthly active principals (a human and each agent actor counted once), connected tenants (a tenant with a SCIM connection or a tenant-scoped inbox), resolved approvals above a free allowance at a small per-unit price, a decision retention tier, and hosted ADS evaluations. Decisions made by the embedded engine are never metered.
7. **Every Cloud connector is a standard wire format or an existing interface.** Identity providers connect as trusted issuers (JWKS or OIDC Discovery), CAEP transmitters and SCIM sources; evidence leaves as OTLP, OCSF, CloudEvents webhooks, signed JWS batches and CSV; delivery is the Chat SDK recipe; agents read the Cloud through a read-only MCP server. No per-vendor npm entry is added for any of them ([0023](/docs/decisions/0023-compose-openapi-ecosystem)). Approvals are never resolvable through the Cloud's MCP server: approver identity comes only from the Cloud's own authentication, and an agent approving an agent's action defeats the guarantee approvals exist to give. The catalog is on the [Cloud integrations](/docs/adapters/cloud-integrations) page.
8. **Position for an acquirer, not a category win.** The realistic upside for an independent authorization company is acquisition by an identity or platform vendor. The consequences are already mostly in place and are now explicit: standards-first wire formats so the Cloud ports into an acquirer's stack; Vercel-native distribution; no authentication product and no gateway; the Cloud repository thin and bound to the interfaces in this repository; the MIT core untouchable ([0021](/docs/decisions/0021-embedded-pdp-hosted-ads)).

## Consequences

- The [Cloud adapter](/docs/adapters/cloud) page reorders its purpose (log, ADS, inbox, SCIM relay), renames its "Audit" row to "Evidence", replaces "considered, not scheduled" with the relay, gains the meters and a lifecycle step for the relay, and closes the "should the sink accept OTel spans" question (no: traces go to your collector, evidence goes to the sink).
- [Audit and observability](/docs/concepts/audit-and-observability) gains an "Evidence and governance" section and resolves sampling.
- New pages: the [SCIM adapter](/docs/adapters/scim), the [SCIM standard](/docs/standards/scim) and the [Cloud integrations](/docs/adapters/cloud-integrations) catalog. `DirectoryStore` joins [extension interfaces](/docs/concepts/extension-interfaces) as the write side of a subject input with a `testDirectoryStore` conformance runner.
- The [roadmap](/docs/roadmap) Phase 2 gains `permdock/scim`, the hosted receiver and the signed export; the Cloud alpha line is reordered; the SCIM receiver leaves the "considered and not scheduled" list.
- [Wire formats](/docs/concepts/wire-formats) gains two CloudEvents `type` values, `dev.permdock.directory` and `dev.permdock.catalog`, for the webhook connector.
- The [threat model](/docs/security/threat-model) gains rows for the SCIM endpoint credential and tenant binding, a compromised relay (bounded to declared `assignable` roles and visible in the sync log), deprovisioning latency, webhook receivers, and the read-only MCP server.
- The [commercial landscape](/docs/research/commercial-landscape) records the exits, the approval commoditisation and the revised meters, so the next reader does not have to rediscover them.
- Invariant 15 in the maintainer guide gains one sentence: the Cloud may relay provisioning into a store the application owns; it still implements neither subject-input interface.

## Alternatives considered

- **Cloud-only SCIM, with the Cloud syncing rows into the application's database.** Rejected: self-hosters would get nothing, and a connector that writes to arbitrary customer databases is a support surface the team cannot carry. An open-source handler with a store interface gives self-hosters the feature and gives the Cloud a single, documented target to relay to.
- **Relax invariant 15 and let the Cloud implement `MembershipSource` behind a cached client.** Rejected: it puts a remote table on the decision path, which is the Aserto architecture and the failure 0021 was written to avoid; a cache does not change who is authoritative during an outage.
- **Keep the inbox as the lead capability.** Rejected: an inbox is a 99-dollar feature elsewhere, and its buyers (developers wiring an agent) are not the buyers with budget (procurement and security). The inbox remains because the store and the token rule are what make an approval trustworthy, and because the Vercel Marketplace template needs it.
- **Broaden into authentication or an identity-aware or MCP gateway.** Rejected for the reasons in Context; recorded here so the question is not reopened without new evidence.
- **Price only on principals and tenants, giving approvals away.** Rejected: delivery and the inbox have a real per-unit cost, and a small price keeps the meter honest without making approvals the product.
- **Sample `granted` events by default to control ingest cost.** Rejected: evidence that omits what was allowed is not evidence; cost is handled by retention tiers.
- **Let agents resolve approvals through the Cloud's MCP server.** Rejected: the approver must be a person authenticated by the Cloud; an agent-resolvable approval is a denial the model can talk its way past.
- **A new JWS `typ` for the relay's bearer.** Rejected: RFC 7523 already defines the assertion; adding a PermDock `typ` would add a row to five pages for no interoperability gain.
